The problem

Great storefront design
is slow, expensive, and fragile

🗓️

Weeks, not hours

A full redesign requires briefing a designer, waiting for mockups, rounds of revisions, and a developer to build it — often 4–8 weeks end-to-end.

🧩

Multi-team dependency

Brand manager, UX designer, front-end developer, and QA — any one of them becoming a bottleneck stalls the whole project.

🔁

Hard to experiment

Testing a new layout, color palette, or seasonal theme means going back to the start. Iteration is expensive, so teams don't do enough of it.

Capabilities

What the AI can do today

🎨

Full visual theme generation

Colors, fonts, border radius, nav style, color scheme — derived from your reference or brief. 10 brand archetypes built in (luxury, organic, editorial, tech, playful…).

📐

Layout & section intelligence

Chooses the right hero layout, product grid density, and section ordering for your category and style. Knows that a luxury brand needs a full-width editorial hero, not a carousel.

✍️

Copy & content population

Writes hero headlines, benefit bullets, testimonial stubs, newsletter hooks, and CTAs — all tuned to your brand voice and archetype.

↩️

Undo / redo & manual override

Every change is tracked. Editors can override any AI decision — colors, text, layout — and walk back 50 steps of history. AI and human co-author the final design.

Design philosophy

AI proposes. Humans decide.

What the AI handles autonomously

Reading and interpreting brand reference URLs or images
Selecting colors, typography, and layout combinations
Writing and structuring storefront copy
Proposing which sections to show and in what order
Applying all changes to the live preview in one step

What stays in human hands

Final approval before anything goes live
Overriding any individual AI decision
Adding feedback to steer the AI's revision
Saving and publishing to the live storefront
Every decision is logged in a full audit trail — nothing is a black box
